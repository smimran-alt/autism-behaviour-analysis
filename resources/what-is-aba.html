What Is Applied Behaviour Analysis (ABA)? A Guide for Parents

Applied Behaviour Analysis (ABA) is an approach to understanding how learning and behaviour are influenced by the environment. It uses observation, data, and individualized teaching strategies to support meaningful skills.

What Does ABA Mean?

Applied Behaviour Analysis is based on principles of learning and behaviour. Practitioners identify important skills, understand what influences behaviour, and use evidence-informed teaching approaches to support progress.

ABA can be used in different settings, including homes, schools, and community environments. Its goals and methods should reflect the learner's preferences, communication needs, dignity, and priorities.

How Does ABA Teaching Work?

An individualized teaching process may include:

  1. Assessment: Understand the learner's current skills, strengths, needs, and preferences.
  2. Goal setting: Choose meaningful, achievable goals collaboratively with the learner and caregivers.
  3. Baseline measurement: Record what happens before introducing a teaching strategy.
  4. Teaching: Use supportive strategies, appropriate prompts, practice opportunities, and reinforcement.
  5. Progress monitoring: Collect data and adjust teaching based on the learner's responses and well-being.

Common ABA Teaching Strategies

Positive Reinforcement

Positive reinforcement occurs when something is added after a behaviour and that behaviour becomes more likely in similar situations. Teaching should use motivating, respectful experiences rather than coercion.

Discrete Trial Training (DTT)

DTT is a structured teaching format that breaks a skill into small learning opportunities. A trial may involve an instruction or cue, the learner's response, and an appropriate consequence or feedback.

Natural Environment Teaching (NET)

NET incorporates learning opportunities into everyday activities and the learner's interests, such as play, meals, and routines.

Prompting and Prompt Fading

Prompts can help learners practice new skills. They should be individualized and gradually reduced when appropriate to encourage independence.

What Skills Can ABA Support?

  • Functional communication and requesting help
  • Play, leisure, and social participation
  • Daily living and self-care routines
  • Learning readiness and academic skills
  • Self-advocacy and making choices
  • Safer alternatives to behaviours that cause harm
Important: Autism is not something that needs to be cured. Support should prioritize autonomy, communication, quality of life, safety, and skills that matter to the individual. Approaches that suppress harmless autistic traits or disregard distress should be questioned.

How Is Progress Measured?

Progress can be measured through observations such as independent responses, frequency, duration, and the level of support needed. Data should be interpreted alongside the learner's comfort, preferences, and meaningful outcomes.

Questions Parents Can Ask an ABA Provider

  • How are goals selected, and how is the learner involved?
  • How do you respect refusal, assent, and communication differences?
  • What happens if the learner becomes distressed?
  • How is progress evaluated beyond compliance or task completion?
  • How do you coordinate with speech-language and occupational therapy professionals?

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ABA only for autistic children?

No. Behaviour-analytic principles are used in education, skill development, organizational settings, and other areas.

Does ABA always use structured table teaching?

No. ABA-based approaches can include play-based teaching, natural routines, and other individualized methods.

How long does ABA take?

There is no universal timeline. The appropriate approach, intensity, and duration depend on individual goals, preferences, progress, and well-being.
