What Does ABA Mean?

Applied Behaviour Analysis (ABA) is an approach to understanding how learning and behaviour are influenced by the environment. It uses observation, data and individualized teaching strategies to support meaningful skills.

ABA can be used in homes, schools and community environments. Goals and methods should reflect the learner's preferences, communication needs, dignity and priorities.

💡 Good support builds skills while respecting the learner's choices and well-being.

How Does ABA Teaching Work?

An individualized teaching process may include:

1📋

Assessment

Understand current skills, strengths, needs and preferences.

2🎯

Goal Setting

Choose meaningful, achievable goals collaboratively with the learner and caregivers.

3📊

Baseline Measurement

Record what happens before introducing a teaching strategy.

4🤝

Teaching

Use supportive strategies, appropriate prompts, practice and reinforcement.

5📈

Progress Monitoring

Collect data and adjust teaching based on responses and well-being.

Common ABA Teaching Strategies

Positive Reinforcement

Positive reinforcement occurs when something is added after a behaviour and the behaviour becomes more likely in similar situations. Teaching should avoid coercion.

Discrete Trial Training (DTT)

DTT uses small, structured learning opportunities involving a cue, a response and appropriate feedback.

Natural Environment Teaching (NET)

NET creates learning opportunities during everyday activities and the learner's interests, including play and routines.

Prompting and Prompt Fading

Individualized prompts support learning and are gradually reduced when appropriate to encourage independence.

What Skills Can ABA Support?

  • Functional communication and requesting help
  • Play, leisure and social participation
  • Daily living and self-care routines
  • Learning readiness and academic skills
  • Self-advocacy and making choices
  • Safer alternatives to behaviours that cause harm

How Is Progress Measured?

Progress can be measured through independent responses, frequency, duration and levels of support. Data should be interpreted alongside comfort, preferences and meaningful outcomes.

Respecting autistic people matters. Autism is not something that needs to be cured. Support should prioritize autonomy, communication, quality of life and safety. Approaches that suppress harmless autistic traits or disregard distress should be questioned.

Questions Parents Can Ask a Provider

  • How are goals selected, and how is the learner involved?
  • How do you respect refusal, assent and communication differences?
  • What happens if the learner becomes distressed?
  • How is progress evaluated beyond compliance?
  • How do you coordinate with speech-language and occupational therapy professionals?

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ABA only for autistic children?

No. Behaviour-analytic principles are also used in education, skill development and other settings.

Does ABA always involve table teaching?

No. Approaches can include play-based teaching, natural routines and other individualized methods.

How long does ABA take?

There is no universal timeline. The appropriate approach, intensity and duration depend on goals, preferences, progress and well-being.
